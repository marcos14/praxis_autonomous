# 10. Planejamentos (Plannings) — PRDs and ADRs with the strategist

The **Planejamentos** screen is the space for PMs, POs and architects: you describe a need and the **strategist** — reading the repositories' code in read-only mode — refines with you a **PRD** (business vision), **ADRs** (architectural decisions) or both, in an iterative conversation. The result is a document grounded in what the system actually is today, ready to become a demand with one click.

## What it is for

- **A complete PRD before the demand**: instead of pasting a raw PRD into Nova demanda (New demand), get there with requirements, acceptance criteria and open questions already settled — fewer question rounds from the analyst, a better plan.
- **ADRs for architecture decisions**: recording context, decision, alternatives and consequences — with the strategist confirming the real constraints in the code.
- **Visualizing the plan**: besides the text, the strategist generates a **visual presentation** (infographics, flowcharts) and, if you ask, a **navigable prototype** of the proposed screens — to align the vision before spending development effort.

## How to use

1. Go to `Planejamentos → Novo planejamento` (Plannings → New planning), choose a **project** or a **group**, the **focus** (PRD, ADRs or both) and the **visual level**:
   - **Documento** (Document) — the `.md` files only;
   - **Apresentação** (Presentation, the default) — plus an HTML with an executive summary, infographics and flowcharts;
   - **Protótipo** (Prototype) — plus a navigable simulation of the proposed screens/flows.
2. Describe the need in your own words. If anything is ambiguous, the strategist asks **decision questions** before writing — answer in the chat.
3. On each turn, the documents appear on the **Documentos** (Documents) tab (with a revision history) and the artifacts on the **Artefatos** (Artifacts) tab (they open in a new tab). Ask for changes in the chat until the document is just right.
4. You can **change the focus and the visual level in the middle of the conversation** — the next turn already follows it.
5. When the PRD is ready, click **Criar demanda** (Create demand): the document becomes the first message of a new demand (with the ADRs attached, when there are any) and the intake analyst takes over from there. The demand card opens right away, and the planning records the link **with the delivered revision**.

### One planning, several demands

The planning is a living document and can generate **as many demands as you need** — starting over from scratch, testing a variant (A/B), delivering in another repository of the group. The **Demandas (N)** (Demands) button at the top opens the handoff dialog, which shows:

- the demands already generated, with the **state** of each one and **which revision** of the PRD/ADRs it received;
- the **drift**: if the documents have evolved since the last delivery, the dialog warns that there are undelivered changes;
- a **warning when there is an active demand** — creating another one from the same plan may produce overlapping work (it is not blocked: A/B testing is legitimate, and the Kanban flags file overlap between demands).

The demand created here is always **complete** (the whole current document). The **complementary** demand — only what has evolved since a completed delivery, with an incremental PRD generated by the strategist — is coming in a future version.

## Working in stages (Product ↔ Architecture)

Planning was designed to be **collaborative and staged** — you do not have to decide everything at creation time:

1. **The PO starts** with the **PRD** focus and refines the document with the strategist until the business vision is settled.
2. **The architect carries on in the SAME planning**: changes the *Produzir* (Produce) field to **PRD + ADRs** (or ADRs only) and sends their points in the chat. The strategist keeps `prd.md` intact, starts producing `adrs.md` — and keeps the two consistent (if an architectural decision contradicts the PRD, it points that out).
3. Each chat message shows **who spoke** (the name of the logged-in user), so the conversation records the whole discussion: what came from product, what came from architecture.
4. Either of the two (with the `demandas.criar` permission) clicks **Criar demanda** when the set is ready — the PRD goes out with the ADRs attached.

The reverse order works too (architect first, product afterwards), and nothing stops a planning from being ADRs-only from start to finish.

## Referências (References — attaching supporting documents)

You attach the material the strategist should take as its basis — an **ADR from another project** to reuse as a template, the **transcript of a meeting** that gives rise to the PRD, drafts, requirement spreadsheets — in three places:

- **at creation** (the *Referências* field of the Novo planejamento form): the very first turn starts from them — Praxis holds the strategist back until the files finish uploading;
- **in the 📎 clip** next to the message field, without leaving the conversation;
- **on the Referências tab** of the open planning, where you can also download and delete them.

It accepts `md`, `txt`, `csv`, `json`, `pdf`, `html` and images (up to 15 MB each).

- Each attachment (and removal) becomes a system message in the chat, and the strategist's next turn receives the list — say in the chat what you want it to do with each one ("use the attached ADR-007 as a format template").
- References are **read-only input**: the strategist consults them but never changes them. They live in the planning folder (the `referencias/` subfolder) and can be downloaded back or deleted at any time.

## Documents and artifacts

- The `.md` files are the **source of truth** and are versioned in the database — every turn that changes a document records a new revision, and you can re-read any older revision.
- The `.html` artifacts are a presentation layer, always regenerated from the documents. They are **self-contained files** (they work offline, with no CDN) written to `PRAXIS_HOME/planejamentos/p<id>/`.
- When you open an artifact, it runs in a **browser sandbox**: the artifact's JavaScript has no access to your Praxis session or to the API — it is presentation only.
- Everything can be **downloaded to your machine**: the documents through the *Baixar .md* (Download .md) button (for the revision on display) and the artifacts through the *Baixar* (Download) button — since they are self-contained, the downloaded HTML works straight from disk, ready to attach to an e-mail or present.

## Engine and model

Plannings use the same engine resolution as the queries (the engine's **Modelo para consultas** field; the creator's user group can pin an engine/model of its own). Since this is analysis and writing work — not production code — a more economical model is usually enough.

## Security

- Access is controlled by the `planejamentos.usar` permission. **Careful**: unlike the Consultas, there is NO anti-code filter here — ADRs cite components, paths and technologies by definition. Grant the permission to people who may see internal architecture details.
- The strategist **never modifies the repositories**: it runs with writing confined to the planning folder, the repositories come in as read-only and, as a final safety net, Praxis compares each repository's `git status` before and after the turn — any change detected fails the turn immediately and lists the files for you to check (nothing is reverted automatically).
- Plannings respect **project access** (ACL), just like the queries: a restricted project does not appear to anyone who has not been granted access.
- Deleting a planning removes the conversation, the documents, the artifacts and the working folder.
